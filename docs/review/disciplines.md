# Reviewing a discipline

A discipline is a rule every orchestrator follows. It's small on purpose.

**1. It's one rule, said in one sentence.**
- Why: a discipline with three ideas gets applied halfway.
- Check: the first line of the body states the rule in a single sentence. If it needs an "and", it may be two disciplines.

**2. It says when it applies and when it doesn't.**
- Why: a rule with no limits gets applied where it hurts. "Mitigate before root cause" is wrong when the mitigation destroys the evidence.
- Check: there's a short "when" and a short "when not".

**3. It shows the mistake it prevents.**
- Why: people and agents recognize a rule faster through what goes wrong without it.
- Check: there's one example of the wrong move and what it caused, written in general terms.

**4. It has no stack.**
- Why: disciplines are shared by every orchestrator and every team.
- Check: no product names, commands or cloud services in the body.

**5. Its name is a noun.**
- Why: the name tells the layer.
- Check: `reversible-first`, `state-is-special`, `verified-impact`. Not a verb.

**6. It's used by at least one orchestrator.**
- Why: a discipline nobody calls is dead text.
- Check: search the orchestrators for its name.
